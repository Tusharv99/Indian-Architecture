Architectural Curators' FAQ

In South Indian Dravidian architecture, the Vimana is the stepped pyramidal tower situated directly over the sanctum sanctorum. In Northern Nagara architecture, the Shikhara is a curvilinear, beehive-like mountain tower crowned by an amalaka.
Squinch arches span the four upper corners of a square room, converting the square into an octagon. This structural transition made it possible to seat colossal circular masonry domes without internal support columns, as seen in Gol Gumbaz.
Known locally as parchin kari, semi-precious gems (lapis lazuli, jade, malachite, coral, and onyx) were chiseled into millimeter-precise floral patterns and embedded flush into polished white Makrana marble without perceptible seams.
